## STRATEGIC FRAMING — READ FIRST. THIS GOVERNS EVERY PART BELOW.

PMCT must NOT be analyzed as another task-management or work-management tool competing
feature-for-feature with Jira or ClickUp. The correct — and commercially strongest — frame is:

```
Jira · ServiceNow · SAP · HR · Finance · Teams        (the project ecosystem — systems of record)
                 ↓
        PM CONTROL TOWER                              (the aggregation, intelligence & control layer)
                 ↓
        CEO · CFO · CTO · PMO                         (decisions)
```

Apply these six standing rules to every part of this document:

1. **Two-layer market map.** Work-management tools (Jira, ClickUp, Asana, Monday, Trello…) are
   the layer BELOW PMCT — systems of record for work. Enterprise PPM / Strategic Portfolio
   Management suites (Planview, Broadcom Clarity, ServiceNow SPM, Oracle Primavera) are PMCT's
   DIRECT competitive tier. The status quo PMCT actually replaces is the invisible glue layer:
   spreadsheets + Power BI + manual PMO reporting + data reconciliation across those systems.
2. **Feature-checklist warning.** Jira/Atlassian and ClickUp have absorbed cross-project
   planning, AI, automation and enterprise governance features. PMCT cannot simply claim
   "we have Gantt + AI + dashboards." Individual features do not differentiate. The analysis
   must test whether the COMBINATION — portfolio prioritization + financial intelligence
   (ROI/NPV/IRR/payback/EVM) + resource capacity + governance + executive reporting +
   configurable enterprise control + deep integrations + self-hosted deployment option —
   creates a sufficiently differentiated product.
3. **Differentiation verdict is mandatory.** The final document must answer, with evidence:
   **DIFFERENTIATION VERDICT: SUFFICIENT / PARTIAL / INSUFFICIENT** — is the combination above
   defensible, or is it a feature list incumbents can replicate?
4. **Coexistence before replacement.** PMCT's wedge is sitting ABOVE Jira/ServiceNow (coexistence),
   not replacing them on day one. Analyze both commercial motions explicitly: coexistence-first
   (integrate, aggregate, prove the layer, expand) versus replacement-first (consolidate tools).
5. **Buyer consequence.** The buyer of a control layer is senior — CEO/CFO/CIO/PMO Director —
   not team leads. This raises ACV, lengthens the sales cycle, shifts the motion to committee
   sales with security review and procurement, and justifies premium pricing. Every pricing and
   GTM conclusion must reflect this buyer, not a bottom-up team-lead buyer.
6. **Pricing consequence.** PMCT prices the LAYER — value delivered (tool consolidation,
   decision speed, financial control, governance, auditability) — never the cheapest seat.
   Undercutting Jira/ClickUp is explicitly the wrong strategy: it signals the task-tool
   category, invites commodity comparison, and destroys the margin enterprise sales requires.

## IMPORTANT COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS RULES

Do not simply say: "PMCT is better."

Instead explain: "PMCT can differentiate through…"

For every differentiation claim ask:

- Does the competitor already have this?
- Is it native or dependent on an add-on?
- Is it available only in an enterprise tier?
- Is it integrated or disconnected?
- Is it configurable?
- Is it executive-friendly?
- Is it financially integrated?
- Does PMCT genuinely provide something different?

Additionally apply the **layer test** to every comparison: does the competitor act as the control
layer ACROSS third-party systems of record (Jira + ServiceNow + SAP + HR + Finance + Teams), or
only within its own data? A rich feature inside one system's walls is not the same capability as
a governed control layer across systems.

Avoid unsupported claims.

## IMPORTANT PRICING RULE

Do NOT attempt to win the market simply by being cheaper.

Analyze: **VALUE × CAPABILITY × TOTAL COST OF OWNERSHIP** rather than LOWEST PRICE.

Anchors: the market spans ≈$5/user/month (entry work management) to ≈$20–30+/user/month
(advanced tiers), with enterprise PPM at custom six-figure contracts. PMCT's pricing must be
value-based on the layer — consolidation, decision speed, financial control, governance,
auditability — positioned premium-to-work-management and value-to-enterprise-PPM. Undercutting
Jira/ClickUp is explicitly the wrong strategy: it signals the task-tool category, invites
commodity comparison, and destroys the margin enterprise sales requires.

Determine whether PMCT should position itself as: Low Cost · Value · Premium · Enterprise — and
explain the commercial implications of each.
